# vol-surface-fitter

An equity volatility surface fitter, built one layer at a time, with each layer
validated before the next is started.

The repository is currently at **Layer 1**: Black-76 pricing, analytic Greeks,
and a suite that cross-checks every closed-form Greek against finite differences.

```
Layer 5   Exotics (autocallable, barrier, digital)          not started
Layer 4   Surface model (SVI, no-arbitrage constraints)     not started
Layer 3   Point-wise implied volatility (numerical root)    not started
Layer 2   Forward construction (spot + dividends + borrow)  not started
Layer 1   Black-76 pricing + Greeks + FD validation         done
```

Each layer consumes the output of the one below it, so the order is not
negotiable.

---

## Why the forward form (Black-76) rather than the spot form

The price of a European option depends only on the risk-neutral distribution of
the underlying at expiry, and that distribution is pinned down by two things:
**where it is centred** — the forward `F`, fixed by no arbitrage — and **how
wide it is** — `sigma`.

Rates, dividends and borrow cost affect only the first. Algebraically, `r` and
`q` never appear separately in the spot-form Black-Scholes formula; they appear
only as `(r − q)`, and the only place that combination goes is into
`F = S·e^((r−q+b)T)`. Black-Scholes with dividends is therefore not a different
model from Black-76 — it is Black-76 with the forward construction hard-coded
into the pricing function.

Factoring it out buys a clean separation of concerns:

```
forward.py    may be arbitrarily complex: discrete cash dividends,
              dividend futures curves, borrow curves, hard-to-borrow spikes
                     |
                     v  a single number, F
bs.py         ten lines, and never changes
```

Swapping the dividend model requires neither touching nor re-validating the
pricer. This also mirrors how valuation teams actually divide responsibility:
market data is one control process, the pricing library is another.

`test_matches_textbook_spot_bs` verifies the equivalence directly.

## What Layer 1 contains

**`bs.py`** — pricing and analytic Greeks, all differentiated with respect to the
**forward**. Accepts scalars or numpy arrays.

| Function | Expression |
|---|---|
| `price` | `df · cp · [F·N(cp·d₁) − K·N(cp·d₂)]` |
| `delta` | `df · cp · N(cp·d₁)` |
| `gamma` | `df · φ(d₁) / (F·σ√T)` |
| `vega` | `df · F · φ(d₁) · √T` |
| `theta` | `r·V − df·F·φ(d₁)·σ / (2√T)` |
| `rho` | `−T·V` (forward rho, F held fixed) |
| `vanna` | `−df · φ(d₁) · d₂ / σ` |
| `volga` | `vega · d₁ · d₂ / σ` |

with `d₁ = [ln(F/K) + ½σ²T] / (σ√T)`, `d₂ = d₁ − σ√T`, and `cp = ±1`.

Both `theta` and the cross Greeks lean on the identity **`F·φ(d₁) = K·φ(d₂)`**.
The same identity is why the two terms generated by `d₁` and `d₂` moving with
`F` cancel exactly in `∂V/∂F`, leaving delta equal to `df·N(d₁)`.

**Vanna and volga are not optional.** On an equity volatility book they are
primary daily P&L drivers; an attribution that stops at delta/gamma/vega/theta
leaves a residual too large to defend.

**`test_bs.py`** — 23 checks in three groups:

1. **Finite-difference cross-validation** (7). Every Greek is compared with a
   central difference of the function it differentiates.
   `test_vanna_is_symmetric_cross_derivative` additionally confirms that vanna
   computed as `∂delta/∂σ` matches vanna computed as `∂vega/∂F`.
2. **No-arbitrage and structure** (9). Put-call parity and the delta parity that
   follows from differentiating it; the requirement that gamma, vega, vanna and
   volga be identical for calls and puts (the parity right-hand side contains no
   `sigma` and is linear in `F`); price bounds; monotonicity in vol; Greek signs;
   the mental-math approximation `C ≈ 0.4·F·σ√T`; equivalence with the spot form.
3. **Degenerate and boundary cases** (7). `T=0`, `sigma=0`, deep ITM and OTM,
   vectorised-versus-scalar consistency, and a sweep over an extreme parameter
   grid asserting that every Greek is finite — **a silent NaN is the most
   dangerous failure mode in a valuation system, because it propagates
   downstream and surfaces as a wrong mark rather than an error.**

## Running it

```bash
pip install numpy scipy        # the only dependencies
python bs.py                   # worked example plus three experiments
python test_bs.py              # validation suite (pytest -q also works)
```

`python bs.py` prints three numerical experiments:

**The Itô correction term.** A Monte Carlo comparison with and without
`−½σ²T`. With it, `E[F_T] = 100.008 ≈ F`, so the forward is a martingale;
without it, `E[F_T] = 102.028 = F·e^{½σ²T}`, the distribution shifts right, and
the call rises from 7.65 to 8.76. **Calls are biased high and puts biased low,
and put-call parity breaks.** The term is not decoration; it is the constraint
that enforces the martingale property.

**ATM forward symmetry.** When `F = K` the log term vanishes, so
`d₁ = +½σ√T` and `d₂ = −½σ√T` are symmetric about zero by construction; raising
`sigma` from 20% to 40% moves them to ±0.20. The same table shows something
counterintuitive: **higher vol makes the option more expensive while making it
less likely to finish in the money** (`N(d₂)` falls from 0.4801 to 0.3446). The
average payoff conditional on finishing ITM grows faster than the probability
shrinks.

**How a forward error becomes a volatility error.** Revising the dividend
forecast from 2% to 3% (S=100, r=4%) moves `F` from 102.02 to 101.01 and the
call price by −0.555 — **1.45 vol points of systematic shift**. Roughly, a 1%
forward error is worth 1.5 vol points, and the sign is asymmetric: implied vol
backed out of calls is pushed one way and puts the other. **Call and put implied
vols disagreeing at the same strike is the signature of a wrong forward, not of
volatility.** Layers 2 and 3 are built around exactly this diagnostic.

## Known limitations

These are deliberate modelling choices, not a to-do list.

- **European exercise only.** American early exercise depends on the *timing* of
  dividends, not just their total; the same forward with different dividend
  schedules gives different American prices. Black-76 cannot express that.
- **`theta` assumes F is fixed and `df` rolls at a constant implied rate.** In
  practice the forward drifts along the dividend and borrow curves, and desks
  differ on the convention. This must be aligned with the official system before
  signing off marks, or P&L explain carries an unexplained constant term.
- **`rho` is a forward rho** and flows only through `df`. The full rate
  sensitivity also passes through `F = S·e^{(r−q+b)T}`. Splitting the two is a
  direct consequence of the forward parameterisation, and arguably its honest
  feature — they are distinct risks.
- **All Greeks are forward Greeks.** The delta a trading desk quotes is usually
  the spot delta, `∂V/∂S = ∂V/∂F · e^{(r−q+b)T}`. The two are close for
  short-dated options and diverge for long-dated, high-dividend or
  hard-to-borrow names. Converting requires the Layer 2 forward construction.
- **A single volatility input.** No skew, no term structure. Removing this
  limitation is the entire point of Layers 3 and 4.
- **Deterministic discounting.** Strictly, `E^Q[F_T] = F` holds exactly under
  the T-forward measure; with stochastic rates there is a rates-equity
  covariance correction, which is also the source of the futures-forward
  convexity difference. Negligible for short-dated equity options.
